Overview

Emittr turns a rooted Android phone into a real, physical USB Human Interface Device: a keyboard, a five-button mouse, and a two-axis scroll wheel, recognized natively by any computer it's plugged into, with no drivers and no software installed on the host side.

It's not remote-desktop software, and it isn't a clipboard-sync trick. The host computer's operating system genuinely believes it has a keyboard and mouse attached, because at the USB protocol level, it does. That distinction is what makes Emittr work in situations where every software-based alternative fails: locked-down corporate machines, air-gapped systems, and even the BIOS screen before an operating system has loaded at all.

The Two Modes of Operation

Emittr is built around two complementary workflows:

  • 1. Direct On-Device Mode: Hold the rooted phone in your hand and type or scroll directly on its touch display using the PWA deck.
  • 2. Local Network Relay (Bridge Mode): Leave the rooted phone plugged in behind the target machine as a dedicated hardware dongle. Connect your everyday laptop, tablet, or unrooted phone to the same local Wi-Fi, open the web deck in your browser (http://<phone-ip>:5000), and type directly from your daily driver's password manager or clipboard. Keystrokes stream over the local LAN and inject into the USB cable.

The technical specification

REPORT 1 · 9 BYTES

Standard 104-key boot keyboard

Full 6-key rollover with a modifier byte for Ctrl, Shift, Alt, and Win, compatible with BIOS, bootloaders, and every OS.

REPORT 2 · 6 BYTES

5-button mouse with horizontal pan

Relative X/Y, vertical wheel, and the official USB HID AC Pan usage for native horizontal scrolling.

Reliability

Emittr watches the USB connection continuously. The moment a cable is unplugged or replugged, every held key and mouse button is force-released, so a dropped connection mid-keystroke never leaves a phantom Ctrl or Shift stuck on the host. A manual "unstick" control is also always one tap away.

Installation

Up and running in three steps, on a rooted Android phone, in under two minutes.

01

Clone & install

Run in phone terminal as root:

phone root 
$ git clone \
  https://github.com/mr-anjaneyam/Emittr.git
$ cd Emittr && bash install.sh
02

Make it stick

Persist across reboots with Magisk:

service.d 
$ cp /opt/usb_hid_deck/02-usb-deck.sh \
  /data/adb/service.d/
$ chmod +x \
  /data/adb/service.d/02-usb-deck.sh
03

Plug in & open

Connect OTG cable, open in browser:

web deck 
→ http://localhost:8088
# or http://hid.keyboard

Use Cases

When a network is locked down, a driver is missing, or a keyboard has simply failed, real hardware still works.

lock

Bypass enterprise restrictions without breaking rules.

Corporate laptops disable USB mass storage, block personal webmail, and restrict the clipboard. But no IT policy bans a standard keyboard.

How Emittr helps: paste text or scripts from your phone, and they arrive as genuine hardware keystrokes. No software installed, no network connection needed.
devices

Leave it plugged in, drive from your personal phone.

Leave your rooted lab phone tethered to the target PC as a hardware bridge. Access the web deck from your everyday personal phone (iPhone or Android) over the secure local network.

How Emittr helps: copy passwords or snippets from your personal vault (Bitwarden, 1Password) and type them straight across the physical USB cable into the target machine.
shield

Diagnose quarantined, air-gapped systems safely.

Incident responders and forensics analysts working on offline or infected machines cannot risk plugging in USB storage drives or installing inspection software.

How Emittr helps: feeds triage scripts, memory collection commands, and system queries strictly through keystrokes, leaving a clean audit trail and zero software footprint on offline devices.
key

Keep master passwords off untrusted clipboards.

Entering 64-character master credentials, PGP keys, or recovery secrets on a client machine risks exposing them to browser extensions, clipboard monitors, or keyloggers.

How Emittr helps: your secret stays in your mobile vault and streams straight into the password field as scancodes. The host clipboard is never touched.
terminal

Control machines before an OS or network boots.

A headless home server, a Raspberry Pi, or a laptop with a dead keyboard stuck in the BIOS setup or GRUB menu.

How Emittr helps: your phone is recognized as a Standard USB keyboard at the BIOS hardware level, allowing full boot menu navigation with zero OS dependencies.
construction

Maintain sealed kiosks, ATMs, and digital signage.

Digital billboards mounted 10 feet up or interactive kiosks sealed behind tamper-proof glass often expose only an external USB extension cable.

How Emittr helps: plug your phone in and use the virtual trackpad, keyboard, and joysticks on the spot, leaving heavy peripheral gear behind.
