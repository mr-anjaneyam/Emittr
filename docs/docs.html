Overview

Emittr turns a rooted Android phone into a real, physical USB Human Interface Device — a keyboard, a five-button mouse, and a two-axis scroll wheel — recognized natively by any computer it's plugged into, with no drivers and no software installed on the host side.

It's not remote-desktop software, and it isn't a clipboard-sync trick. The host computer's operating system genuinely believes it has a keyboard and mouse attached, because at the USB protocol level, it does. That distinction is what makes Emittr work in situations where every software-based alternative fails: locked-down corporate machines, air-gapped systems, and even the BIOS screen before an operating system has loaded at all.

The technical specification

REPORT 1 · 9 BYTES

Standard 104-key boot keyboard

Full 6-key rollover with a modifier byte for Ctrl, Shift, Alt, and Win — compatible with BIOS, bootloaders, and every OS.

REPORT 2 · 6 BYTES

5-button mouse with horizontal pan

Relative X/Y, vertical wheel, and the official USB HID AC Pan usage for native horizontal scrolling.

Reliability

Emittr watches the USB connection continuously. The moment a cable is unplugged or replugged, every held key and mouse button is force-released — so a dropped connection mid-keystroke never leaves a phantom Ctrl or Shift stuck on the host. A manual "unstick" control is also always one tap away.

Installation

Up and running in three steps, on a rooted Android phone, in under two minutes.

STEP 1

Clone & install

Run the installer from your phone's terminal, as root:

git clone https://github.com/mr-anjaneyam/Emittr.git cd Emittr bash install.sh
STEP 2

Make it stick

Keep it active across phone reboots with Magisk:

cp /opt/usb_hid_deck/02-usb-deck.sh /data/adb/service.d/ chmod +x /data/adb/service.d/02-usb-deck.sh
STEP 3

Plug in & open the deck

Connect with an OTG cable, then open in the browser:

Direct URL http://localhost:8088 or http://hid.keyboard

Use Cases

When a network is locked down, a driver is missing, or a keyboard has simply failed — real hardware still works.

weekend

Control your PC from across the room.

Leave your phone plugged into the computer as a bridge, then hand control to whatever phone is actually in your hand — over Wi-Fi, from the couch.

How Emittr helps: the plugged-in phone keeps acting as a genuine USB keyboard and mouse, while you drive it remotely from any device on the network.
bolt

Get a document onto a locked-down computer.

The PC in front of you has USB drives disabled, blocks personal email, and won't sync your clipboard.

How Emittr helps: paste your text on your phone and it arrives as real keystrokes — no file transfer, no network dependency, nothing to block.
mic

Dictate into any app, on any machine.

Your phone has excellent speech-to-text. The desktop in front of you has none.

How Emittr helps: speak on your phone, and every sentence streams straight into whatever you're writing.
emergency

Rescue a machine with no keyboard.

A home server, a Raspberry Pi, or a laptop whose keyboard just died — and you can't even get past setup.

How Emittr helps: your phone is recognized before the operating system even loads, right at the BIOS.
